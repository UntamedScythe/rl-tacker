# NeedBoost Design Manifesto — AI Coaching Platform

## Core Mission

NeedBoost is **not** another Rocket League statistics website.

NeedBoost is **an AI coach.**

Statistics exist only to support coaching.

Every feature, every visual element, and every interaction should answer one question:

> **"What should this player practice tonight?"**

If a design decision makes the interface look more impressive but makes the coaching less clear, choose coaching every time.

---

# Product Philosophy

NeedBoost exists because replay trackers stop at analytics.

NeedBoost begins where replay trackers end.

Other products answer:

> **What happened?**

NeedBoost answers:

> **Why did it happen?**
>
> **Why does it matter?**
>
> **What should I do next?**

The player should leave every report feeling:

> **"I know exactly what to practice tonight."**

—not—

> **"Those were interesting statistics."**

---

# Emotional Goal

The product should never make players feel criticized.

It should make them feel coached.

NeedBoost never says:

> "You're bad."

Instead it says:

> "This habit is costing you games, and here's the fastest way to fix it."

The report should leave players excited to queue another game.

Not discouraged.

---

# Governing Principles

## Every pixel either teaches something or gets out of the way.

Nothing exists purely for decoration.

Every interface element should either:

* teach
* guide
* reassure
* explain
* coach

Everything else should disappear.

---

## Statistics are evidence—not headlines.

Charts support coaching.

Numbers support coaching.

Graphs support coaching.

The coaching is the product.

The statistics are simply proof.

---

## Coaching always comes first.

Whenever there is a choice between

* adding another graph
* adding another statistic
* improving the coaching

always improve the coaching.

---

# Visual Identity

NeedBoost should feel like opening an elite tactical replay workstation.

Inspiration:

* Formula 1 telemetry software
* Arc Browser
* Linear
* High-end sports analytics
* Professional esports coaching tools

Avoid:

* generic Tailwind dashboards
* gaming RGB
* cyberpunk aesthetics
* overused AI gradients
* neon everywhere
* esports logo clichés

The atmosphere should communicate:

* precision
* confidence
* intelligence
* momentum
* restraint

---

# Surface Design

Instead of flat gray cards:

Create layered surfaces.

Background

↓

Base surface

↓

Raised surface

↓

Interactive surface

Each layer separated by:

* 1px borders
* subtle lightness differences
* restrained depth

No heavy shadows.

No excessive gradients.

---

# Geometry

Use precision details sparingly.

Examples:

* one clipped corner
* telemetry dividers
* subtle dot grids
* engineering spacing
* mono section labels

These details should make the interface feel engineered rather than decorated.

---

# Typography

Typography should do the heavy lifting.

Large headlines.

Clear hierarchy.

Plenty of whitespace.

Avoid filling space simply because it exists.

Luxury software uses restraint.

---

# Hero Philosophy

The homepage should explain the product in under three seconds.

The hero is **not** a search form.

The hero is a coaching moment.

Instead of leading with inputs:

Lead with a coaching insight.

Example:

> **You're winning the boost fight.**
>
> **You're losing the positioning battle.**

Supporting evidence:

> In your last 10 games you collected 847 boost—
> top 15% for your rank.
>
> But you spent 41% of your boost chasing plays your teammates already covered.

Immediately underneath:

Search by:

* Name
* Player ID
* Replay Upload

The product should already make sense before the user searches.

# Coaching Card

The coaching card becomes NeedBoost's signature component.

Every coaching card contains three sections.

## Observation

Plain English.

Example:

> You're arriving to second challenges late because your rotations are wider than they need to be.

---

## Evidence

Small mono text.

Example:

Average boost:

41

Defensive third:

18%

Pad pickups:

Low

---

## Practice

Specific action.

Example:

Spend 15 minutes in Free Play collecting only small pads while staying supersonic.

---

Every coaching card should answer:

What happened?

Why does it matter?

What should I do next?

---

# What To Practice Tonight

This becomes one of the most important sections.

Not:

Improve rotations.

Instead:

Tonight:

* Spend 15 minutes practicing small-pad routes.
* Rotate immediately after first touch.
* Avoid chasing corner boost during defensive transitions.

The player should finish reading and immediately know what to practice.

---

# Statistics

Statistics should have hierarchy.

Not every number deserves equal importance.

The top coaching insight determines which statistics are emphasized.

Everything else becomes supporting information.

The player's eye should naturally move:

Insight

↓

Evidence

↓

Charts

---

# Confidence

Never overstate conclusions.

If replay evidence strongly supports something:

Speak confidently.

If evidence is weaker:

Acknowledge uncertainty.

Trust is more valuable than sounding certain.

---

# Product Voice

NeedBoost sounds like an experienced coach.

Not an analytics engine.

Not ChatGPT.

Not Ballchasing.

Example:

Instead of:

> Boost starvation detected.

Say:

>
> This isn't a mechanics issue.
>
> It's a routing habit.

# Technical Philosophy

Do **not** redesign working systems.

No backend changes unless necessary.

Prioritize:

* UX
* clarity
* coaching
* product identity

before adding more features.

---

# The Acid Test

Every feature should pass one question:

> **Does this help the player know what to practice tonight?**

If the answer is no—

remove it,
simplify it,
or redesign it.

# Final Goal

NeedBoost should not be remembered because it has the prettiest dashboard.

It should be remembered because it makes players better.

When someone closes the browser, they shouldn't remember a radar chart.

They should remember one sentence:
